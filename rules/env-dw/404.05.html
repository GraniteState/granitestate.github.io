Env-Dw 404.05 Additional Design Standards

Effective 2024-12-25.

Env-Dw 404.05 Additional Design Standards. (a) In addition to the design criteria specified in this part, requirements for: (1) Surface water filtration, disinfection, and waste recycling shall be as specified in Env-Dw 716; and (2) Source water treatment for corrosion control shall be as specified in Env-Dw 714. (b) Site selection, yield and drawdown testing, and source water protection for wells for large CWSs and water quality for new wells shall be as specified in Env-Dw 302. (c) Standards for well abandonment shall be as specified in We 600 as adopted by the New Hampshire water well board. (d) Standards for submersible pumps shall be as specified in We 700 as adopted by the New Hampshire water well board. (e) Standards for installation, maintenance, repair, or replacement of well pumps shall be as specified in We 700 as adopted by the New Hampshire water well board. (f) The total developed groundwater source capacity shall equal or exceed the design maximum day demand and equal or exceed the design average day demand with the largest producing well out of service. (g) The source water protection plan for surface water sources, which may be an existing plan, as described in Part 3.1 of the document adopted by reference at Env-Dw 404.04(a), shall: (1) Include: a. A map showing the delineation of the watershed area contributing water to the source on an appropriate scale applying standard delineation methods and identifying smaller priority areas, including riparian buffer zones, priority sub-watersheds, or both, that present higher vulnerabilities to source water quality; b. A watershed characterization summarizing physical and natural features, land use, and known waterbody conditions, including pollutant sources and water quality use impairment(s); c. An inventory of known and potential contamination sources, including all point and non-point pollutant sources having the ability to discharge or release contaminants to the source or its tributaries within a minimum 6-hour travel time from the intake; d. A determination of susceptibility to contaminant sources that considers, among other things, the distance, quantity, solubility and toxicity, dispersion, and dilution of contaminants and review of pertinent compliance monitoring data reported by a PWS; e. A list and description of prioritized and practicable protection actions having identified milestones to be completed by the owner, committed partners, or both, which shall include best management practice (BMP) inspections within a minimum of 4,000-feet upgradient of the intake within the watershed; f. A list of recommended protection strategies to be considered by the owner; g. A public education program targeted to watershed residents; and h. A schedule with dates for periodic plan updates and evaluation; (2) Be approved by the department pursuant to Env-Dw 404.03 prior to placing the proposed surface water source into operation; and (3) Be implemented by the owner or partner subsequently identified in the plan, over a specific timeframe as set forth in the department’s approval pursuant to (2) above. (h) Isolation valves shall be located at not more than 500 foot intervals in areas where the ground elevation is below the flood hazard elevation as determined in accordance with (o) below and in areas where water pipes are at a risk of being damaged by erosion. (i) Run time meters shall be installed on all pumps. (j) Water meters shall be: (1) Installed to continuously measure the flow from: a. Each source independently; b. Any washwater; c. Any recycled water; d. Any blended water of different quality; and e. The finished water at the distribution entry point prior to the first customer and after any storage or clearwell at the pumphouse or treatment facility; and (2) Maintained in accordance with the standards of the AWWA as required by Env-Dw 402. (k) Data from the meters required by (j) above shall be recorded such that the operator may periodically access and save the data for tracking purposes. For each source, the monthly volume of withdrawal and the 24-hour maximum volume for each month shall be recorded and reported to the department in accordance with Env-Wq 2102. (l) A sampling point shall be provided after the last point of chemical injection with consideration given to adequate mixing. If atmospheric storage is located prior to the first customer, sampling points shall be provided both upstream and downstream of the atmospheric storage tank prior to the first customer. (m) Any PWS that conducts a partial or full replacement of a lead service line or galvanized service line requiring replacement shall be subject to the requirements of 40 CFR 141 Subpart I, 7-1-24 edition. (n) Handling and disposal of lead pipe, galvanized pipe that might contain lead, or other lead components shall be subject to Env-Hw 400, Env-Hw 500, Env-Hw 800, and Env-Hw 1200. (o) In addition to (p) below, the flood hazard elevation shall be determined using: (1) The freeboard value, reached by adding 3 feet to the 1-percent-annual-chance flood elevation, or adding 3 feet to the highest flood elevation of record, whichever is higher; or (2) A best available scientific approach that uses readily available, actionable hydrologic and hydraulic data and methods that integrate current and future changes in flooding based on latest climactic predictions. (p) If the project is for infrastructure within at least one of New Hampshire’s 17 coastal zone municipalities, including Dover, Durham, Exeter, Greenland, Hampton, Hampton Falls, Madbury, New Castle, Newfields, Newington, Newmarket, North Hampton, Portsmouth, Rollinsford, Rye, Seabrook, and Stratham, determination of the flood hazard elevation specified in paragraph (o) above shall address projected storm surge, sea level rise, and precipitation events identified in the “New Hampshire Coastal Flood Risk Summary – Part I: Science and Part II: Guidance for Using Scientific Projections" prepared for the New Hampshire Coastal Flood Risk Science and Technical Advisory Panel 2019 available as noted in Appendix B. A representative concentration pathway (RCP) of RCP 4.5 or higher and a “low” or “very low” tolerance for flood risk shall be applied. (q) If a proposed component of a drinking water system is proposed to be located below a flood hazard elevation as determined using the approaches in paragraph (o) above and there are no practicable alternatives to the proposed site, measures shall be taken to minimize the risk of flood damage. The designer shall document the mitigating measures or design modifications that will be taken to reduce the threats from locating the project below the flood hazard elevation. (r) No component of a drinking water system shall be located in a Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)-designated floodway, with the exception of buried piping. (s) To the extent practicable, drinking water facilities shall not be located in areas subject to a risk greater than 1% from earthquakes, fires, or other disasters that could damage the CWS or a portion thereof.
