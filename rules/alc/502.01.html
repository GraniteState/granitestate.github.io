Alc 502.01 Ethical Requirements

Effective 2026-07-13. Expires 2036-07-13.

Alc 502.01 Ethical Requirements. (a) Licensees and CRSWs shall practice within their respective statutory scopes of practice as follows: (1) For MLADCs, the scope of practice set forth in RSA 330-C:10; (2) For LADCs, the scope of practice set forth in RSA 330-C:11; (3) For LCSs, the scope of practice set forth in RSA 330-C:12; and (4) For CRSWs, the scope of practice set forth in RSA 330-C:13. (b) Licensees and CRSWs shall be bound by the principles of the NAADAC code of ethics, effective June 1, 2025, as referenced in Appendix B. (c) In addition to the scope of practice requirements listed in (a) above, licensees and certificate holders shall abide by the following ethical standards with regard to: (1) Quality of care, the licensee or certificate holder shall make a commitment to providing the highest quality of care for the client; (2) Non-discrimination, the licensee or certificate holder shall not discriminate against any persons based on race, color, religion, age, sex, marital status, national origin, ancestry, sexual orientation, political affiliation, or mental or physical disability; (3) Professional responsibility, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Exercise professional judgment; b. Maintain the best interest of the client; and c. Assist the client toward the primary goal of recovery; (4) Professional competence, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Provide competent professional service to the client; b. Participate in ongoing professional education to maintain state-of-the-art knowledge and skill; c. Be responsible for their conduct in all areas of professional life; d. Recognize and maintain personal and professional boundaries and limitations; e. Seek the advice and counsel of colleagues and supervisors whenever such consultation is in the best interest of the client; and f. Recognize the effect of impairment on professional performance and be willing to seek appropriate treatment when necessary; (5) Professional standards, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Not claim or imply professional knowledge, qualifications, certifications, or affiliations that they do not possess; and b. Not lend their name to, or participate in, any professional or business relationship, which might misrepresent or mislead the public in any way; (6) Professional obligation to the public, the licensee or certificate holder shall not state or imply a higher degree of knowledge or insight into co-occurring or substance use disorders that would be available through similarly situated or trained professionals; (7) Publications, the licensee or certificate holder shall preserve the integrity of the profession by acknowledging and documenting any materials or techniques used in creating their opinions or in writing, editing, or publishing papers, pamphlets, or books; (8) Client welfare, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Place the best interest of the client before conflicting professional commitments or professional gain; b. Always provide an appropriate setting for the work within their scope of practice; and c. Provide accommodations for maintaining a supportive environment for any client having special needs; (9) Confidentiality, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Not reveal information relating to a client unless the client consents to such release of information in writing and after consultation with the licensee or certificate holder; and b. Preserve client records and information regardless of the media used to store such information; (10) Client and public safety, notwithstanding confidentiality rules, the licensee or certificate holder may reveal confidential information to public authorities or other professionals to the extent that they reasonably believe it necessary to prevent a client from serious harm whether self-inflicted or inflicted upon a third person, or where the client is in imminent danger, or in danger of injuring another person; (11) Client relationships, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Respect and maintain an objective, non-possessive, non-personal, professional relationship with the client at all times; b. Obtain the client’s permission for recording a session, for involving any third party in a session, or for releasing any information pertaining to the client; c. Not enter into a business relationship with the client during the professional relationship; d. Not enter into a business relationship with any other person if doing so would adversely affect the client; e. Not engage in any sexual activity with any current or former client; f. When it is in the best interests of a client, release or refer the client to other programs or individuals as dictated by professional standards and ethically responsible practice; g. Not enter into a professional relationship with a client if the licensee’s or certificate holder’s own responsibilities to or relationship with a third person would adversely affect the client; and h. Not enter into a professional relationship with anyone with whom the licensee or certificate holder has had a previous intimate relationship; (12) Professional integrity, the licensee or certificate holder shall: a. Cooperate with and adhere to Alc 502; b. Never knowingly make a false statement in a professional capacity; c. Promptly report violations of this professional code of conduct by other licensees or certificate holders to the OPLC; and d. Disclose to the OPLC any material fact which could adversely affect a professional-client relationship, to include any disciplinary action taken by any other board or regulatory body; (13) Remuneration, the licensee or certificate holder shall not accept fees or gratuities for professional work from a client whose fees are being paid through an institution or agency and who is otherwise entitled to such services; and (14) Professional promotion, the licensee or certificate holder shall maintain and promote the integrity and advancement of the alcohol and drug profession. (d) With regard to remuneration, LADCs and MLADCs shall establish reasonable financial arrangements based on fees customarily charged in their locality for similar services.
